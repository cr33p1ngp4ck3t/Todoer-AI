## How licensing and payment work

The [data-partner program](https://datafactor.com/data-partner) describes an initial scope discussion and estimate, legal/security review, approved read-only connections, extraction, removal of sensitive identifiers, quality review, licensing, and payout. Scope includes the systems, people, repositories, and time periods the company authorizes. The final written agreement determines compensation, timing, permitted use, retention, and restrictions.

A non-exclusive license allows further licenses if the agreement permits them. An exclusive license reserves the agreed rights for a buyer for the specified scope and duration. Neither description by itself transfers ownership of the company or its software.

The case-studies page claims 30–60 days from first conversation to payment. Treat that as a published timeline, not a guarantee. The [website terms, section 3](https://datafactor.com/terms), explicitly say automated scores and payout estimates are non-binding and that actual terms require a signed written agreement.

## Implications for Todoer AI

The user-provided report rates the repository 73/B and estimates $80. This is an estimate, not approved compensation. The large case studies concern extensive historical archives and scarce data, so they are not direct price comparisons for one solo repository.

The report identifies about 13 potentially mineable engineering changes. Real features and bug fixes paired with regression tests, reproducible setup, and genuine review discussions improve the substance available for evaluation. Renaming the repository addresses its generic presentation but does not create production usage or additional engineering history.

Several accounts operated by one person remain one human contributor. Switching author identities to imply separate people would misrepresent the history and adds no genuine collaboration. Keep attribution accurate and invite real contributors for useful work if collaboration is wanted.
